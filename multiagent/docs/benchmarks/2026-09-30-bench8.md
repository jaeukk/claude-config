# bench8: vanilla vs. the multiagent skill, before and after the 2026-09-30 fixes

Run 2026-09-30. The raw record (plan with amendments, driver, prompts, every run's transcript list, contamination
inspection, blind adjudication) is local and not in git: `~/.claude/multiagent/tasks/2026-09-30-bench8/`. The
answer key and grader are in its `hidden/` folder. **Before a rerun, move `hidden/` out of the installation**:
conductors launched in `multiagent/` can list `tasks/`.

## Summary

- On a fact-check with no executable oracle, a session using the skill scored the same as a vanilla session
  (n = 3 per arm; every verdict was "not distinguishable"). At `1f71d78` it cost 1.6× vanilla without an audit and
  2.0× with one, almost all of it ceremony turns.
- Eight conductor sessions exposed four defects and most of the overhead. They were fixed in `881935b`/`d2d0eb4`
  (defects) and `c5203ce`–`cdedb47` (performance). Re-measured at `cdedb47`:
  - Claude tokens fell 70% without an audit and 65% with one.
  - The skill now costs 1.09× vanilla without an audit, and 1.31× plus about 49k Codex tokens with one.
- When the Codex review completed, it caught the one error that single sessions usually missed: in 2 of 2
  completed reviews before the fixes, and 3 of 3 after.

## Design

- **Task.** A 2,975-word literature note on Kim & Torquato, arXiv 2504.16924, to be fact-checked against the paper's
  LaTeX source. It has 12 planted errors in low-salience details (methods parameters, captions, equation forms, a
  table cell, three arithmetic claims) and 27 correct controls, 12 of them traps. The prompt says some statements may
  be wrong and never gives the count.
  - A first version (simple substitutions in a 1,100-word note, "bench7") and a pilot of a second version both
    scored 12/12 for every run and were hardened.
- **Arms.** All are real headless Claude Code sessions: Opus 5.5, effort high, team account, `--permission-mode auto`.
  - **V (vanilla):** the skill is blocked.
  - **M (multiagent, no audit):** the prompt says to use the skill with `audit_cycles` 0.
  - **R (multiagent, one audit):** one audit cycle, with the review definitions supplied.
  - The M and R conductors run from `multiagent/` and make their own routing decisions.
- **Grading.** A regex grader, then a blind adjudication of every reworded or uncredited line (the adjudicated score
  is primary).
- **Validity.** Every transcript written during a run was scanned for forbidden paths and inspected by hand before
  grading. Two runs (M3, R3) read another task's contract and were rerun as M3b and R3b.

## Results at `1f71d78` (before the fixes)

| Arm | Errors fixed (of 12) | Controls (of 27) | Claude USD | Turns | Wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V | 11, 11, 12 (11.33) | 27 | 0.82 | 7 | 89 s |
| M | 11, 11, 11 (11.00) | 27 | 1.30 | 17 | 178 s |
| R | 12, 12, 11 (11.67) | 27 | 1.67 + ~55k Codex tokens per review | 33 | 279 s |

Only E11 discriminated. It claims about 20 (2D) and 30 (3D) wavevectors are constrained; the true count is 40 and 60
(or 20 and 30 *independent* ones). V fixed it 1 time in 3, M 0 in 3, and R 2 in 3. R3b's conductor backgrounded its
critic and ended the session, which killed the review, so R3b missed it.

**Defects found** (8 conductor sessions):
- A critic was left running in the background when the session ended (review lost, usage unrecorded).
- Six of 8 conductors left a stale `tasks/.active-task`; the hook made it impossible for them to clear it.
- Conductors read other tasks' contracts as templates.
- A critic brief could not be written mid-task.
- 23 shell-rule refusals, over half of them false positives (a quoted `>`, `2>&1`, `/dev/null`).

## Results at `cdedb47` (after the fixes)

| Arm | Errors fixed | Claude USD | Claude tokens | Turns | Wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V (unchanged) | 11.33 | 0.82 | 308,883 | 7.0 | 89 s |
| M before → after | 11.00 → 11.00 | 1.30 → 0.90 | 1,203,656 → 363,206 (−70%) | 17.3 → 7.7 | 178 → 104 s |
| R before → after | 11.67 → 12.00 | 1.67 → 1.08 | 2,492,297 → 865,388 (−65%) | 32.7 → 13.0 | 279 → 181 s |

Codex tokens per completed review went from 55,296 to 49,160. Every run kept all 27 controls. The pre-registered rule
(turns and USD down at least 25%, quality within 1) was met for both arms.

What changed in behavior:
- No M conductor created a contract.
- Every R conductor used `policy_engine.py review` in the foreground.
- No stray pointer was left.

## Lessons

- Cost scales with turns × context: every turn re-sends the paper, the skill and the history. Cutting ceremony turns
  saved about 2/3 of the tokens. Shortening the skill text (3,530 → 2,103 words) was a small part of that.
- Opus 5.5 saturates planted-error fact-checks unless an error hinges on a definition stated elsewhere. A benchmark
  that ranks review against no review needs more errors of that kind and more runs.

USD figures are Claude Code's list-price estimates (`total_cost_usd`), not billing. Codex tokens are reported
separately and never added to USD.
